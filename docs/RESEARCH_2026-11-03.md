# Sealwork and Accord: research and product decisions

Research observed November 3, 2026. This is a dated assessment, not a leaderboard or prediction of the judges' votes. Sources below were read directly unless an access limit is stated. Repository documentation supports descriptions of what a team claims to have implemented; it does not mean their tests, deployments or benchmarks were independently rerun.

## Recommendation

Improve Sealwork around one complete job: a bookkeeper imports invoices and bank receipts, reviews combined and ambiguous allocations, and exports a confidential reconciliation report with independently checked balances. Keep the technical payment and verification machinery behind that job. Build Accord separately around software-contractor milestones: a private invoice is payable only to the agreed recipient, inside the agreed budget, after the exact agreed GitHub delivery is checked.

A generic spending firewall is crowded. Adding a firewall, privacy wallet, swap screen, treasury, marketplace and chatbot would make either product harder to understand and verify. The credible differentiation is a useful, complete workflow with visible exceptions and recovery. This is a product judgment, not an official scoring rule.

## What the official event asks for

The [live HACKATHON.md](https://nearhackathon.com/HACKATHON.md) specifies development from November 1 through **November 14, 2026, 23:59 UTC**, judging starting November 16 and results by November 18. Q-001 allows work from the October 19 announcement; earlier work must be identified as baseline. The prize pool is $250,000 paid in NEAR: one $55,000 overall grand prize and $10,000/$3,000/$2,000 placements in each of 13 tracks.

The guide is more useful than promotional summaries for implementation decisions:

- Q-010: testnet-only projects are eligible in all tracks. Mainnet is necessary only for an integration that exists solely on mainnet.

- Q-012: a reproducible local project with a public repository and demo video is acceptable. A hosted walkthrough is a plus and should remain available through judging.
- Q-015: at most **two different, independent and mutually disclosed projects per person**. Original and improved Sealwork are versions of one project. A second repository does not create a second eligible Sealwork entry. The intended separate products here are Sealwork and Accord; existing Facet/Kiosk work is preserved and must not be silently counted as additional entries.
- Q-017: Claude Code and Codex screen reproducibility and claimed features and prepare preliminary reports. **Humans decide**; a low AI score alone cannot reject an entry.
- Each project/repository is submitted once. The final application needs exact human-provided identity/contact/payout fields, explicit consent and an approved payload. This work does not submit an application or publish a social post.

The [October 19 announcement](https://www.near.org/blog/near-hackathon-build-beyond-boundaries) frames the event around programmable money and AI. Its older one-team-submission phrasing differs from the current guide; the current Q-015 governs this assessment. No public weighted rubric was found. ### Relevant tracks and implications

[Privacy & Private AI](https://github.com/nearai/near-hackathon-prompt-examples/blob/main/tracks/03-privacy.md) asks for confidential computation, private financial information and private inference. This is Sealwork's strongest fit. The official example uses fictional financial documents and evidence of verified hardware, so a clear sample workflow is more useful than requiring a reviewer to upload real customer accounts.

Agent Economy asks for agents that earn, hire and pay for work. Sealwork's processing worker actually earns a reserved fee after checks. AI Security & Defense concerns spending authority, policy and evidence. Sealwork's exact-build records contribute, but should not be sold as a generic agent-money defense. Autonomous Business is a better fit for Accord's bounded contractor-payment workflow. Real-World Money is plausible only with honest testnet/native-NEAR labeling; neither product currently settles bank dollars or production stablecoin invoices.

## What NEAR is, and how its current direction matters

NEAR remains a proof-of-stake smart-contract blockchain. Its [architectural overview](https://docs.near.org/protocol/network/architecture) explains accounts that hold balances, keys and optionally contracts; parallel processing across shards; receipts for communication; validator staking; and NEAR reserved against onchain storage. Native NEAR pays gas and storage and secures the network. These are deployed protocol functions, separate from the broader ecosystem's services.

The current [NEAR homepage](https://near.org) positions NEAR as **“The Currency of Agents”**, emphasizing confidential intelligence, cross-chain execution and user ownership. It does not mean every NEAR transaction is private or every contract autonomously uses AI. Homepage chain/performance statistics are marketing claims, not measurements from this assessment.

| Primitive/service | What it provides | Limit that affects these products | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| NEAR accounts and contracts | Named accounts, keys, balances and deterministic state/payment rules | Public state remains public; an upgrade/full-access authority remains powerful | Separate testnet deployments and explicit authorities |
| [Function-call access keys](https://docs.near.org/protocol/access-keys) | Key scoped to a receiver and method names; restricted calls attach no NEAR | Key allowance pays **gas**, not a recipient-payment spending cap; a contract must enforce business budgets | Accord places payment policy in the treasury contract |
| [Transaction lifecycle](https://docs.near.org/protocol/transactions/transaction-execution) | Asynchronous receipts, cross-contract calls and callbacks | A scheduled transfer is not proof that the recipient was paid; failure and pending callbacks need distinct states | Reserve before checks; confirm payment or show retry/claim state |
| [NEAR Intents](https://docs.near-intents.org/) | Users express desired outcomes; solvers supply cross-chain execution | Q-007 says no Intents testnet exists; a quote does not itself prove settlement | Do not add swaps to reconciliation; defer Accord cross-chain payout until the core loop warrants it |
| [Chain Signatures](https://docs.near.org/chain-abstraction/chain-signatures) | NEAR-governed signing for external-chain transactions | Signing capability does not authenticate an external chain's balances or delivery facts | No decorative Bitcoin demo; not needed for current native testnet workflow |
| [NEAR AI Cloud verification](https://docs.near.ai/cloud/verification) | Private inference with attestation and response-signature evidence | Verify freshness, signer/policy/model and response bindings; a TEE signature does not make invoice contents true | Optional private suggestions; label original AI evidence separately from human edits |
| [OutLayer NEAR integration](https://app.outlayer.ai/docs/near-integration) | Confidential WASM execution, project secrets, pinned versions, callbacks and execution evidence | Infrastructure, hardware, keystore and source/API availability remain dependencies; use actual limits and binding fields | Pin worker/verifier binaries and reconcile their responses against the funded job |

NEAR is useful here because financial records can be processed privately while deterministic payment rules remain inspectable. Ordinary reconciliation software could run this arithmetic offchain. The additional proposition is a checked confidential work product and a worker fee conditional on that result. Whether bookkeepers value that enough to adopt it still needs customer evidence.

## Customers and ordinary software alternatives

[Xero's bank reconciliation page](https://www.xero.com/us/accounting-software/bank-reconciliation/) already describes matching suggestions, corrections, explanation and summaries. Sealwork is not novel merely because it suggests a receipt-to-invoice match. Its plausible wedge is a private, independently checked CSV work product that can complement an existing ledger. It should not try to replace an entire accounting system.

[Ramp](https://ramp.com/) and [BILL](https://www.bill.com/) represent existing invoice/AP/approval alternatives for Accord. Vendor descriptions and performance claims are not independent customer or accuracy evidence. A crypto-native software team with NEAR funds and GitHub delivery milestones is a narrower first user than “all companies”. Native testnet NEAR is not production payroll, a bank connection or compliant multi-country AP.

No user interview, customer adoption, processing accuracy on real books, or willingness-to-pay study was conducted in this session. A completed test suite is implementation evidence. Product-market fit remains unproven.

## Why these Sealwork changes follow from the research

The old product already had confidential processing and conditional worker fees. The weak point was the bookkeeper's full workflow: suggestions were difficult to correct, ambiguous items could only be left behind, and the report did not export useful partial balances and receipt-level unresolved amounts.

The separate version adds local exact matching as a fast optional convenience, editable allocations with required reasons, approval invalidation after edits, currency-separated totals, partial balances and separate invoice/receipt exports. The private result commits to the original source files. The verifier checks balances and receipt records as well as allocations. Unknown ledger references now fail instead of disappearing. Spreadsheet exports neutralize formula-leading text without changing the JSON originals. Large expanded reports are refused before funding rather than failing after a fee is reserved.

AI remains a proposal source, not an accounting authority. Original AI evidence identifies the original suggestion; manual edits have their own approved-proposal fingerprint. The Rust verifier runs separately from the worker but shares its reconciliation implementation. This reduces delivery tampering; it is not an independently authored arithmetic implementation or audit. The checker establishes consistency with supplied records, not invoice authenticity or bank provenance.

## Grand-prize assessment

The available evidence does not support an honest numerical probability or a definitive “Scrim wins” leaderboard. Focus, demonstrable completion, clear NEAR necessity and useful outputs are controllable. Competitor breadth is not a reason to imitate every track. Leash has a direct, legible safety story; Sealwork must make its customer result equally clear and prove the private-work/payment loop with current evidence. Improving that loop is a better bet than accumulating features.

The next decisive evidence is a bookkeeper finishing a realistic batch, understanding the remaining exceptions, exporting the result and recovering it later—followed by customer feedback. For Accord it is a fixed contractor receiving a payment for an exact approved delivery, while wrong commit/recipient/duplicate/budget cases fail and an interrupted payment remains recoverable.
